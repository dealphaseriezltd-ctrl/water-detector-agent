⚖️ What these detectors can — and cannot — do

What they do: PQWT, ADMT and similar instruments measure tiny natural electrical signals in the ground along a profile, then their built-in software draws a colourful cross-section and points at "water zones" with depth numbers.

The problem: no published, peer-reviewed physics connects those signals to aquifer depth or yield. The algorithms are proprietary black boxes — you cannot see raw data, and two operators can get two different "answers" on the same ground.

  • ✅ Useful as reconnaissance — a quick first look, especially where you have nothing else.
  • ⚠️ Not a substitute for resistivity (VES), seismic, or a logged borehole nearby.
  • 🚫 Never drill — and never pay — on the device's word alone. That is exactly the trap The Borehole Doctor exists to close.

How to use this app: log what the device says, then let the second opinion stress-test it against your terrain and nearby borehole records. If the story doesn't hold, run a VES before the rig arrives.
